What Is a Showrunner in TV? The Complete Definition

What is a show runner? A showrunner is essentially the head honcho of a TV series, with final say in creative decisions. In TV, they outrank the directors of individual episodes. Unlike in the movie business, a TV showrunner is the driving force behind the series.

The term "showrunner" came about to differentiate the hands-on executive producer from others or as a title of respect, but don't actually make the key decisions.

What is a show runner in practical terms? These creative leaders ensure that every episode is consistent, of high quality all while handling the business side of TV production.

What Does a Showrunner Do? Core Responsibilities Explained

To get what does a showrunner do, you need to look at all the different parts of their job. It's a mix of leadership and business smarts. Their tasks cover just about every aspect of TV production.

Creative Leadership and Vision

Showrunners are basically storytellers who decide the direction of their show's stor, and the underlying themes. They work closely with writers, and directors members to create a narrative that flows. This includes

  • Running script development and making key plot calls
  • Keeping the show's tone and style consistent
  • Breaking down episodes and handing out script assignments
  • Giving each script a final review before production

Management and Production Oversight

The showrunner definition also involves a lot of management responsibilities. They're in charge of budgeting and getting different departments to work together. At the same time, they serve as the main point of contact between the team and the executives.

The Path to Becoming a Showrunner

What is a showrunner in TV without knowing how they got there? Most showrunners work their way up the TV writing ranks.

  • Staff Writer - Starting out in the writers' room
  • Story Editor - More experienced writer with more responsibilities
  • Executive Story Editor - Senior writer role
  • Co-Producer - Handles writing and some production tasks
  • Producer - Production duties take center stage
  • Supervising Producer - Top production spot
  • Co-Executive Producer - Right-hand person to the showrunner
  • Executive Producer/Showrunner - The person in charge

It takes years of hard work and making connections in the industry to get to this point. The Writers Guild of America even has a training program for showrunners.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a showrunner and a director?

A showrunner is in charge, creatively speaking, of the entire series. They manage multiple episodes and guide the directors who work on each one.

Can a showrunner also be the creator of a show?

Many showrunners create their own shows, but some creators hire experienced showrunners.

What does a showrunner do day-to-day?

Showrunners juggle a lot. They oversee writers' rooms, make key creative calls, and stay in touch with network bigwigs.

What skills are essential for becoming a showrunner?

To be a successful showrunner, you need solid writing skills, and a good understanding of business. You also need to be able to handle the challenges of managing the team.
